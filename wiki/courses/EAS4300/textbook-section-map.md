## Edition confirmed: 2nd

Despite the other filename in the source folder claiming "1st Edition, 1970," **this book is the 2nd
edition.** The preface states it directly ("In the preparation of this second edition…") and thanks
Nicholas Cumpsty, John Denton, Edward Greitzer, Sir William Hawthorne, and Herbert Saravanamuttoo —
all 2nd-edition-era reviewers. It is signed "Churchill College, Cambridge, P.G.H."

**This matches the syllabus's specified edition (ISBN 9780201146592, 2nd ed).** Section references in
the syllabus can therefore be taken at face value — with the specific exceptions flagged below.

**Page offset for the PDF:** printed page + 12 = PDF page index. (Printed p.3 = PDF index 15.)

## The four things to ask the instructor

1. **Turbofans — 5.5 or 5.6?** The syllabus says 5.6, but 5.6 is Turboprop/Turboshaft. Turbofans are
   5.5. Either the reference is off by one, or turboprops are also in scope. *(Practical answer: read
   5.5 for turbofans; skim 5.6, since turboprops appear nowhere else in the schedule.)*

2. **Lecture 22 — is it 8.7 or Chapter 9?** §8.7 is "Turbine and Compressor Matching"; centrifugal
   compressors are Chapter 9. Given L21 already covers 8.6 Turbine Performance, and the topic is
   named "Centrifugal Compressors," **9.1–9.2 is the near-certain intent.**

3. **Lectures 25 and 26 — Chapter 9 or engine-level material?** Both cite Chapter 9, which is entirely
   about the centrifugal compressor. So "Testing and Performance Characteristics" (§9.5) most likely
   means **centrifugal compressor performance maps, surge, and choke**, not gas turbine engine test
   cells. Similarly "Course Takeaways" at 9.1–9.2 reads like leftover centrifugal material.
   **This is the biggest open question in the schedule** — it changes what's on the final.

4. **Are shocks examinable?** §3.7 Shocks sits just past the assigned 3.1–3.6. But normal and oblique
   shocks are indispensable for §6.3 Supersonic Inlets (Lecture 10), which *is* assigned. Almost
   certainly covered in lecture even if the reading reference stops at 3.6. **Treat shocks as in
   scope.**

## What the course skips

Worth knowing so you don't over-study:

- **Ch 4 as a *lecture topic*** (Boundary Layer Mechanics and Heat Transfer) — but §7.6 Boundary Layer
  Limitations leans on its concepts. ⚠️ **Correction 2026-09-05**: a HW1 problem from a prior
  Anup Mannem-taught offering (same instructor as your syllabus) asks for a wake-momentum-deficit drag
  coefficient — genuinely Ch. 4 material — so **do not assume Ch. 4 is untestable** just because it
  has no dedicated lecture slot. Basic momentum-deficit/drag-coefficient calculations from a given
  velocity-defect profile are fair game even without a full boundary-layer-theory lecture behind them.
- **§1.3 Rockets, §1.5 Turbojets/Turbofans/Ramjets** — the qualitative intro survey
- **§5.6 Turboprop and Turboshaft Engines** (unless the L08b reference is literal)
- **§6.6 Supersonic Combustion** — no scramjets
- **§7.9 Radial Equilibrium, §7.10 Subsonic Axial Compressor Design**
- **§8.8 Turbine Stage Design**
- **Chapters 11–14 entirely** — chemical rocket thrust chambers, propellant combustion, turbopumps,
  electric propulsion. **Rocket coverage is Chapter 10 only**, i.e. vehicle-level performance
  ($I_{sp}$, the rocket equation, staging, missions) rather than thrust-chamber internals.
