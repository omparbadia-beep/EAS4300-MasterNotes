## Course goals

Introduction to processes in propulsion systems for aircraft applications, focused on **gas turbine
components, systems testing, and systems analysis**. By the end you should be able to:

- Explain the principles of **inlets, compressors, combustors, turbines, and nozzles**
- Evaluate each component's performance **within an integrated propulsion system**
- Apply propulsion system testing methods and basic control strategies
- Use analytical methods to assess and interpret the performance and design of modern aircraft engines

**ABET coverage:** *High* — identify/formulate/solve complex engineering problems using engineering,
science, and mathematics. *Low* — communicate effectively with a range of audiences.

## Course arc — how the pieces fit

The course is built as **fundamentals → cycle analysis → component-by-component → rockets**:

1. **L01–L05 · Toolbox.** Thrust, efficiencies, control-volume conservation laws, combustion
   thermodynamics, compressible gas dynamics. Nothing here is propulsion-specific yet; it's the
   machinery every later chapter runs on.
2. **L06–L09 · Cycle analysis (the engine as a black box).** Treat the engine as a thermodynamic
   cycle with ideal or component-efficiency-corrected stations. Ramjet → turbojet → turbofan, then
   how the engine and airframe match.
3. **L10–L13 · Non-rotating components.** Inlets, combustors, afterburners, nozzles. Where the
   efficiencies assumed in step 2 actually come from.
4. **L14–L22 · Turbomachinery.** Axial compressors, transonic fans, axial turbines, centrifugal
   compressors. Velocity triangles and Euler's turbomachinery equation dominate.
5. **L23–L26 · Rockets, testing, synthesis.**

The single most reused idea in the course is the **station-numbered control volume**: pick stations,
apply conservation of mass/momentum/energy between them, and use stagnation properties to carry the
kinetic energy along. See [station-numbering](../concepts/station-numbering.md) and
[stagnation-properties](../concepts/stagnation-properties.md).
